# Self-review — soft-line-motion-v2

Exact output: artifacts/render/soft-line-motion-v2/draft.mp4, 90 frames, 30fps, 1280x720, 3.000s, silent. Full FFmpeg decode passed in finalize.py. Looked at the 9-frame encoded contact sheet, the 45-frame ordered sheet (every 2nd frame) and full-size stills of frames 8, 25 and 40 from the Remotion export. This is sampled inspection by the producing agent, not continuous playback and not human review.

What improved against v1 (observed in frames): one continuous line from rear foot through hip to head; boxing stance with weight on the rear leg; hip rotation with each jab; guard hand stays up; contact at f25 reads as jab meeting a parry; head follows the body without the old locked circle. Numeric: outline finite, reverse-seek identical, grounded foot slide worst 1.0 (red, 32 frames over 0.6) and 0.9 (black, 3 frames) units per frame, closest fist-to-forearm 12.3, fist-to-head-surface 64.6.

Known weaknesses: the story is jabs against a repeated block, no counter or slip (black does not strike back); black comes from a karate block drill so its stance is upright and the pairing feels mismatched; red's far (guard) arm collapses into a short stub when attached to the body line; the 2D projection foreshortens arms that move out of the side plane; red shows slight foot skate; both fighters' jabs are on the source's fixed 16-frame rhythm, so the second block is not timed to a landing punch. Torso still has a mild S-curve from the spline through chest and shoulder joints.

Lessons (project-only): (1) front-view bvh2clip output is the wrong projection for side-view fights; (2) shoulder and hip joints are 25-50 units off the spine centerline in side view, so limb strokes must be attached to the body line or they float; (3) the CMU root joint is the pelvis, not the hip joints, so torso length must be measured from the hip-joint midpoint; (4) a smooth spline through captured joints removed the need for hand-tuned elbow rounding. No global rule change. Creative acceptance pending; not a reference-parity claim.
